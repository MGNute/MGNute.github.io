This post is basically an informal narrative telling of my career journey so far because it's taken some weird turns over the years. Here is my official [C.V.](/Michael_Nute_CV_Public.pdf)

### Undergrad: 2001-2005

I went to Cornell for undergrad and did a double major in Math and Econ. I went in planning just to major in math, although my Dad had studied Econ in college and pushed me to dabble a little bit. That was how I ended up taking Stats and Econometrics though which ended up having a big influence on everything since. One thing about Cornell that I really appreciated was the huge variety of courses that anyone could just sign up for, so among other things I took horticulture, metal shop, lasers, cooking, aquaculture, a French film course, et cetera. 

### First Jobs: 2005-2012

My first job after college was in management consulting. I was actually hired by a small, boutique consulting firm that specifically did pricing strategy projects, mostly for B2C clients, although right when I started they were acquired by a much larger firm called Monitor which at the time had a thriving marketing practice. That was a really cool first job. My first big client there was an office products distributor where we redesigned their whole approach to their price points and rebates to their dealers. I did a bunch of smaller projects for some medical product clients and another distributor. Two of those were pricing new product launches which was fascinating. I learned a lot at Monitor but probably the biggest thing was how to use data to tell a story, which they do not teach in college.

I had taken a couple of actuarial exams during my Senior year and after about two years I decided to go back to that track, so I took a job at a Property/Casualty Insurance company called The Hanover Insurance Group in their actuarial department. I started off doing pricing for commercial property and liability products and then rotated to corporate risk management which I did for three years. That was mainly doing capital adequacy and capital allocation modeling, but also catastrophe risk modeling, plus some predictive modeling work for the personal auto product. I rotated again to managing a four-person team doign commercial auto pricing for a bit, although at the time the company was active in M&A and I got the chance to join that team which was a great opportunity. That was a lot of due diligence, financial modeling, analyzing deal opportunities and then in one case actually implementing a transaction with the operations team. 

Throughout my time there I was taking exams to get my actuarial fellowship, although the winter of 2011 I decided to at least apply to Ph.D. programs in Statistics because I had been kicking that idea around for a while. I was really unsure of whether to actually leave and do it though, but ultimately when I got into UIUC I didn't feel I should turn that down. So I left in August 2012 to start my Ph.D.

### Grad School: 2012-2019

I really enjoyed graduate school. It was challlenging in its own way and it was a lot of work, and it's interesting to think of what might have been if I had stayed in insurance, but if nothing else grad school was fun. We had 9 new Ph.D. students that year and damn was that an impressive group to be part of. A few of those folks have tenure at R1 schools now, a couple are with FAANG companies now and one is at one of the national labs. 

At the end of my second year I had a couple of fortunate breaks in a row. The first was that I got an internship with Caterpillar where I had to program a big end-to-end ETL pipeline to feed a dashboard in real time. I had some SQL experience going in but had never *really* written software, so this forced me to learn python and good coding habits. That turned out to be lucky because I got the chance to work with a CS faculty who had just moved her lab from Texas to UIUC, and I was able to be productive in that right away strictly because of the python I learned a few months earlier. 

That professor (Dr. Tandy Warnow) ended up becoming my advisor, and I spent the rest of my time there working on algorithm development for biological sequence data. I ended up really enjoying that research. I did some theoretrical stuff about estimation algorithms under different statistical models, but also some real nuts and bolts algorithm development for multiple sequence alignment and for microbiome characterization. I had the pleasure of collaborating with another faculty member in Anthropology (Dr. Becky Stumpf) who studied the microbiome of human and non-human primates, and I ended up on a primatology paper during grad school as a result. I ended up getting a fellowship to dig deeper on that research so I stayed an extra two years to work on it (although that was partly because my wife and I had three kids in that time, with the last one coming in early 2019). 

### Postdoc Research & Family Chaos: 2020-2026

After grad school my wife had been doing well with her career at P&G, so we lived in Cincinnati and I did some consulting work and looked for opportunities there. In the Spring of 2020 though remote work became suddenly very fashionable, and I ended up getting a (remote) postdoc offer with a PI at Rice University that I had worked with previously. That involved microbial genomics algorithms which was the reasearch I really wanted to do, so I jumped on that and it worked out quite well.
 
We got a ton of papers out those first couple years, including some really high profile ones like Emu and a review paper on deep-learning methods. I ended up winning the Postdoctoral Research award from the Engineering College in 2022 (kind of like a really niche oscar for Best Postdoc). 

In early 2022, I had the opportunity to co-found a startup with another postdoc at Rice. Co-founding a startup had been a goal of mine since I first went to grad school, so I jumped at the chance and that eventually moved us to Boston in mid 2022. We had a small amount of capital to work with although most of the early time we spent refining our investor pitch and doing customer discover work, but also building a code base that could be reused and maintained over time. In 2023 though my oldest daughter got sick and I had to take a lot of time off to manage the logistics of her treatment, so halfway through 2023 I left the startup. I was fortunate that my old PI offered me the postdoc job back, which came with enough flexibility that I could actually work and care for Ellie. We were also fortunate that when her treatment ended at the end of 2023 she was in complete remission and there hasn't been any recurrence since. 

Since I came back to academia I've been promoted to Reearch Scientist and we've continued to do a lot of good work. We implemented a synthetic DNA sequence screening system internally at Rice and we won 2nd place in a DARPA competition for pathogen detection. We've also continued to get some cool papers out.
